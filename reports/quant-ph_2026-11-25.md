### Abstract

This thesis investigates the role of nonlinearity in approaches beyond standard quantum mechanics, following a path from modifications of quantum dynamics to the possibility of an emergent statistical description. We first examine Weinberg's nonlinear formulation and its implications for the statistical interpretation of quantum states. We then discuss de Broglie-Bohm theory and the Madelung formulation, which provide a framework for comparing guided trajectories with ensemble descriptions involving momentum fluctuations. The Hall-Reginatto exact uncertainty approach identifies the fluctuation energy associated with the quantum potential.
  We then investigate whether quantum dynamics can emerge from the collective statistics of solitons in a fluctuating background, using a Galilean formulation of the complex sine-Gordon model. Within a weak-noise approximation, we identify a regime in which the statistics of the soliton centres reproduce the Schr{ö}dinger equation, with an effective action scale determined by the background fluctuations. This correspondence is established for Gaussian ensembles and potentials at most quadratic.
  These results provide a concrete setting for exploring how quantum dynamics can emerge as a statistical description of underlying nonlinear field dynamics.

**Authors:** Hannah Seabrook, Fabio Costa, Paul Skrzypczyk, Giulia Rubino

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31365v1

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31365v1

**Submitted:** 2026-11-23

### Abstract

Indefinite causal order allows processes in which the order of events is not predetermined, enabling information-processing advantages unattainable with fixed causal structures. A long-standing open problem is whether such processes can be certified without trusting the devices used to demonstrate them, analogous to device-independent certifications of Bell nonlocality. Causal inequalities offer a route towards this goal, since their violation is intended to rule out all definite causal structures. However, fully device-independent certification of indefinite causal order remains elusive. The difficulty lies in the closed laboratories assumption, which requires each party to interact at most once with systems that mediate communication. Without this assumption, ordinary two-way communication can reproduce causal-inequality violations without involving indefinite causal order. Yet an untrusted device could violate the assumption by interacting with the mediating system more than once, while remaining undetected from input-output statistics alone. We address this loophole by embedding a minimal set of additional experimental components into a certification scheme. These elements probabilistically probe the underlying process, enabling the closed laboratories assumption to be validated directly within the protocol. Our framework provides operationally meaningful minimal-assumption tests of indefinite causal order, clarifying its distinction from nonlocality and paving the way for cryptographic and self-testing applications.

### Abstract

Entanglement and magic are usually regarded as independent quantum resources. Here we connect the two in the toric code, where the stabilizer Rényi entropy of the stored logical state, a widely used measure of magic, acquires a direct interpretation in terms of entanglement. We introduce the spinal reflected entropy, defined on a neighborhood of a spine of the underlying surface as the reflected entropy between a disk around the vertex of the spine and bands along its noncontractible loops. The result extends to surfaces of higher genus and to general two-dimensional topological stabilizer codes on qubits. Logical magic thus becomes a geometrically defined entanglement quantity, one that shallow circuits preserving the code space cannot change.

### Abstract

We develop a framework for preparing and transporting coherent quantum samples of nonreversible Markov chains. Our approach uses spectral filtering to construct state-dependent phase operations, which are combined with fixed-point amplitude amplification to transport quantum states along a sequence of related chains. We study two complementary constructions with different oracle requirements and target states. The first uses coherent access to both forward and time-reversed transitions and targets the stationary quantum sample, with a filtering cost governed by a multiplicative-reversibilization gap. The second requires only forward transitions and selects the leading eigenstate of a flat discriminant, which may differ from the stationary quantum sample; we quantify this bias under additional spectral and reversibility-on-average conditions. For the forward-only construction, we implement the spectral filter using generalized quantum signal processing with a single signal qubit, apart from transition registers and oracle workspace. We establish a common transport procedure and derive error and resource bounds that account for spectral properties, overlaps between successive selected states, and the costs of coherent multi-step transition oracles. Up to logarithmic factors, spectral-filter degrees scale inversely with the square roots of the relevant spectral gaps, while transport introduces an additional inverse-square-root dependence on certified lower bounds on squared overlaps between successive selected states. Our results extend existing nonreversible spectral methods to multistage coherent state transport with controlled approximation errors.

### Abstract

Decoherence, which arises from the interaction of a quantum system with a typically much larger thermal environment, or bath, poses major challenges to the controllability of quantum systems. The dynamics of such open quantum systems is often represented by Markovian quantum master equations of the Lindblad or Redfield type. These equations describe the dynamics of the open system with a sum of coherent (Hamiltonian) and decoherent (dissipative) contributions. It is often assumed that coherent controls only impact the coherent contribution, while the decoherent contribution is taken to be time- and control-independent. However, the decoherent contribution must also be control-dependent if coupling to a thermal bath is to relax the system to a Gibbs state at thermal equilibrium, which is given in terms of the system's Hamiltonian. In this paper, we investigate the effect of accounting for control-dependent decoherence on coherent control protocols. To this end, we examine the effect of accounting for control-dependent decoherence on the controllability of an open quantum system in the case of a two-level system coupled to a thermal bath. We show that coherent controls wield greater influence on the dynamics of the system than predicted by a Lindblad-type equation that models decoherence as time- and control-independent.
