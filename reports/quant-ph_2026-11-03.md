### Abstract

We revisit the pNRQCD open-quantum-system description of bottomonium in a quark-gluon plasma, writing the full master equation in a spherical basis and establishing a framework for its numerical treatment. The equation does not rely on an expansion in the ratio of the binding energy over the temperature, while retaining the Born, Markov, and dilute approximations. It depends on suitable convolutions of Coulombic Green's functions with real-time chromoelectric correlators, rather than a finite set of transport coefficients. It contains jump-operator contributions that enter with both positive and negative signs, so it is not of a Lindblad type. We utilize optimal operator rotations to minimize the negative sector and define a positive-sector approximation that retains only the optimized positive terms, obtaining in this way a Lindblad equation that can be simulated efficiently with the open-source code QTraj for arbitrary chromoelectric spectral functions. We treat the discarded sector as a systematic uncertainty associated with positivity-breaking effects of the pseudo-Lindblad generator. Positivity breaking effects can be attributed to the approximations made in the derivation of the quarkonium master equation, such as the Born--Markov approximations. Using a spectral model constrained by known infrared and ultraviolet limits, we compute the in-medium width and Bjorken-evolved survival probabilities of the $Υ(1\mathrm{S},2\mathrm{S},3\mathrm{S})$. The computations show the convergence of the binding-energy-over-temperature expansion at high temperatures and provide the first large-scale quantum-trajectory simulation results for bottomonium in the low temperature regime. We can thus follow the bottomonium evolution over the full temperature range relevant in heavy-ion collisions.

### Abstract

Real-time quantum simulation of field theories offers access to scattering and decay dynamics, circumventing the limitations of Euclidean lattice methods. However, representing bosonic fields on qubits requires a finite-dimensional approximation and can entail costly multi-qubit operations. Hybrid qubit-qumode architectures avoid this by encoding bosonic fields directly in oscillators. We formulate scattering in the (1+1)-dimensional Yukawa theory for such a device, with staggered fermions on qubits and the scalar on qumodes. Trapped ions are a natural candidate, with the fermions on the internal states of the ions and the scalar on their collective motion, so that the Yukawa coupling becomes a native spin-dependent displacement. Emulating the device with tensor networks, we study the decay of a single scalar particle and fermion-antifermion scattering around the resonance region, using observables that can be measured on the device natively. We compare the results with those obtained using lattice perturbation theory. The emulation is able to resolve corrections beyond the Dyson summation of the one-loop self-energy.

### Abstract

Characterizing local Hamiltonians that host interesting many-body states as eigenstates has been an important problem in quantum many-body physics. For matrix product states (MPS), there is a well-known construction of parent Hamiltonians that have the MPS as the frustration-free ground state. However, recent results on quantum many-body scars have shown that such a construction does not exhaust all possible Hamiltonians that have the MPS as an eigenstate. In this work, we develop an exhaustive construction of generalized parent Hamiltonians that have a given translation-invariant MPS as an eigenstate. These go beyond the conventional projector-based construction of frustration-free parent Hamiltonians, where each local Hermitian term has the MPS as an eigenstate, by allowing non-Hermitian telescoping terms that cancel out with periodic boundary conditions (PBC). We derive a local tensor equation that establishes a correspondence between inequivalent classes of generalized parent Hamiltonians, i.e., those that cannot be related using linear combinations with frustration-free Hamiltonians, and the MPS transfer matrix spectrum. We illustrate the connection to intrinsic physical properties, including non-Abelian symmetries and zero correlation length, and further extend this construction to block-injective MPS. Our framework hence provides a systematic bridge between generalized parent Hamiltonians and the local structure of quantum states.

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00272v1

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00272v1

**Submitted:** 2026-10-30


### 摘要

### Abstract

Quantum linear regression models, such as quantum extreme learning machines and quantum fidelity kernels, are trained on features that can only be estimated from finitely many measurement shots. Sampling noise is usually treated as a nuisance that more shots suppress; we show instead that it can fundamentally reshape the instabilities of the learning problem. As the number of measurement outcomes grows, the prediction error displays a triple-descent profile, with two peaks of fundamentally different origins. The first, classical, peak occurs when the measurement first becomes informationally complete, is a direct analogue of double descent in classical linear regression, and is caused by ill-conditioning of the measurement itself. This peak affects predictions only through their variance, and is thus suppressed by increasing the number of shots at inference. The second, quantum, peak occurs beyond informational completeness when the number of outcomes matches the number of training states: shot noise populates unphysical outcome combinations and the regression fits these pure-noise features with large weights, corrupting the learned model itself. More training or test shots cannot remove this quantum peak, which disappears only in the ideal limit of exact training features. Using random-matrix theory, we derive the shape of both peaks analytically, accounting also for ridge regularization. Our results show that treating quantum features as exactly known overestimates classical instabilities while missing genuinely quantum ones.
