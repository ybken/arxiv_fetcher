**Authors:** Yu-Hao Chen, Renata Wong

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13998v2

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13998v2

**Submitted:** 2026-11-10

### Abstract

Adaptive variational algorithms such as ADAPT-VQE and AVQITE screen an operator pool at every growth step, and ADAPT-VQE also re-optimizes all parameters. We study a pool-free alternative for variational imaginary-time evolution (VITE). The layers are fixed in advance by nested commutators of the Hamiltonian, grouped by symmetry and particle number, and the next layer is appended when the relative McLachlan residual exceeds a threshold. On five non-integrable systems (the mixed-field Ising chain and tori, and stretched H$_4$), this dynamic VITE reaches a relative error of $10^{-4}$ with 1.6-5.4 times fewer circuits than the cheapest of six to eight adaptive variants, including single-Pauli-string and qubit-excitation pools and batched ADAPT-VQE. On the chain the advantage persists from 8 to 16 sites. Screening and re-optimization account for most of the adaptive methods' cost. On the spin models, a longitudinal-field scan shows that the advantage requires broken integrability, which makes adaptive screening expensive. The advantage is lost on transverse-field Ising models, at tight targets (relative error $10^{-6}$) and where the schedule front-loads weak layers.

### Abstract

The zero-point energy of an $LC$ circuit measures the polarization fluctuation of an insulator coupled to it. In a crystal this fluctuation is the bulk quantum weight $K_{\rm bulk}$, which the Chern number $C$ bounds below by $|C|$. We show that a finite Chern flake adds an edge term that survives as the flake grows. The low-frequency transitions of its chiral edge have squared dipole matrix elements that sum to an edge weight $K_{\rm edge}\ge |C|$ per unit area, a floor that follows from the positive absorption of both circular polarizations and the known cancellation of circular dichroism between edge and bulk. At weak coupling, which a growing flake eventually leaves, a circuit slower than the edge modes reads $K_{\rm bulk}+K_{\rm edge}$, averaged over two field directions, and a faster one approaches $K_{\rm bulk}$, so the reading depends on whether the flake is made large or the circuit slow first. In exact free-fermion calculations on lattice flakes with $|C|=1$ and 2 the summed circular dichroism of the edge sector extrapolates to $|C|$ in magnitude, so that the floor is $|C|$. Above the floor, each edge channel adds a number fixed by the boundary shape and its edge velocities, which square and rectangular lattice flakes with one channel follow to within 0.003, while the circular dichroism counts only the net chirality. The avoided crossing of the circuit with the edge mode gives the edge weight in units of the Hall conductance quantum, up to a geometric factor of the capacitor.

### Abstract

Optical trapping of neutral atoms is almost universally described as an electric-dipole (E1) effect. We show that the magnetic field of light, acting on a magnetic-dipole (M1) transition, can induce an ac Zeeman shift significantly larger than the ac Stark shift, thereby enabling magnetic trapping. ### Abstract

Krylov complexity has become a central probe of operator growth, scrambling, and quantum chaos. Yet, a basic fact hinders its experimental study: laboratory quantum systems are routinely driven in time, whereas the Krylov construction assumes a fixed generator and breaks down once the Hamiltonian varies. Here, we solve the time-dependent many-body Krylov problem exactly and in closed form for scale- and rotation-invariant systems realized in current quantum-simulation and control platforms. We find exact relations that map the driven dynamics onto mutually orthogonal Krylov subspaces and yield a closed-form spread complexity, determined solely by the spectral properties of the initial Hamiltonian and the driving schedule. The construction extends to Liouville space and solves the time-dependent density-matrix Krylov problem, which makes the Krylov dynamics of initial thermal states experimentally accessible. In scale-invariant quantum gases, such as the driven Lieb-Liniger model, the Tonks-Girardeau gas, and the Calogero-Sutherland model, the complexity is set by the width of the atomic cloud and its rate of change, directly accessible via in situ and time-of-flight imaging. In rotation-invariant spin systems such as the Haldane-Shastry and central-spin models, it is governed by collective spin precession that can be probed and read out in trapped-ion, cavity-QED, and circuit-QED platforms.

### Abstract

How much memory does a quantum device need to prepare systems one at a time? We address this question through the lens of quantum resource theories, which formalize physical restrictions on how the memory may interact with the stream of systems it processes. By comparing sequential with collective preparation, we show that under general assumptions both have the same asymptotic memory cost per output. Still, sequential preparation can require an unboundedly larger memory than collective one, and we investigate how this gap depends on the physical restrictions on the allowed interactions. For energy-conserving unitaries, we determine both optimal memory dimensions needed for preparing pure states from an energy eigenstate, and find that the extra memory required sequentially either remains bounded or grows logarithmically with the number of outputs. Under noisy operations, we reveal transformations for which one auxiliary qubit suffices for preparing arbitrarily many independent outputs collectively, while no memory of fixed dimension can deliver them sequentially. Finally, for sequential protocols that repeat a fixed interaction, we show that a $d$-dimensional memory that succeeds for $d^2$ rounds will succeed forever, and that such a sequential preparation is equivalent to quantum catalysis.

### Abstract

Quantum reinforcement learning (QRL) has emerged as a promising approach for controlling quantum systems and accelerating learning tasks with quantum resources. However, most existing QRL formulations still retain a classical reinforcement learning interface: states and actions are often encoded in computational bases and rewards are extracted through measurements. This interface is restrictive for genuinely quantum environments: choosing a measurement can discard entanglement information, while repeated intermediate measurements collapse the evolving state and require costly re-preparation. This motivates a fully QRL framework in which the agent, environment, rewards, and return accumulation are all quantum. In this paper, we take a step toward this goal and introduce a new QRL framework in which both the policy and the environment are modeled by conditional quantum channels. We then propose a quantum policy-gradient algorithm for parameterized conditional quantum channels with a trainable control basis. We also derive finite- and infinite-horizon gradient estimators using a new parameter-shift rule. This rule is needed because policy parameters are shared across repeated interaction blocks and the infinite-horizon objective is not a finite circuit; rather than shifting every occurrence and evaluating the whole trajectory, our estimator shifts only one sampled policy block. Sampling this block from a geometric distribution yields an unbiased gradient estimator under bounded rewards. Finally, we establish convergence of the resulting stochastic gradient-ascent procedure to stationary points under standard smoothness and step-size assumptions.

### Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) have recently demonstrated the ability to generate parameters for variational quantum circuits without conventional optimization. However, the conditions under which this approach is effective remain unclear. Here, we investigate a frozen, general-purpose LLM that generates complete circuit parameter vectors from symbolic circuit and Hamiltonian descriptions, with optional iterative energy feedback. ### Abstract

Pauli propagation simulates quantum circuits and many-body dynamics by evolving observables in the Heisenberg picture as sparse sums of Pauli strings. As Pauli-string operations reduce to bit manipulations, its performance is limited by memory bandwidth and capacity rather than computational throughput.
