### Abstract

Generative AI can be a powerful tool for supporting the design and implementation of quantum applications, whereas the burden of checking whether generated circuits realize the intended computation increases. In this study, we regard this burden of human understanding, checking, and judgment as ``trust cost,'' and propose a development methodology that integrates AI and formal verification to reduce it. **Submitted:** 2026-10-10

### Abstract

We give an explicit treatment of the third tensor power of the Clifford representation on $n$ qutrits, for $n\geq2$. This representation has five pairwise inequivalent irreducible constituents, with multiplicities $1,1,1,1,2$. The decomposition reduces the Clifford twirl of a tensor cube to four real parameters. Two of them are spectral moments of the input. The other two are cubic invariants expressed as sums of cubes of the Weyl characteristic function and the discrete Wigner function. On trace-one Hermitian matrices, the invariant polynomials of degree at most three are spanned by the constant function and four algebraically independent functions. These functions do not separate Clifford orbits. For pure inputs a single parameter suffices. We recover its range and the characterization of its maximum by stabilizer states. We bound the stabilizer fidelity, determine full separability across the three copies in the symmetric family, and recover the criterion for a complex projective $3$-design. We classify the jointly invariant quantum Boolean functions. For stabilizer testing and for testing unknown observables we give explicit bounds on copies and queries. We also give character formulas for the finite projective Clifford groups and record the complete character table for two qutrits.

**Authors:** Hansol Kim, Younghun Kwon

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14778v1

**Submitted:** 2026-10-10

### Abstract

When implementing scalable fault-tolerant quantum computing using superconducting quantum processors, one of the critical challenges to overcome is the presence of defective qubits arising from fabrication and calibration imperfections. A defect on a specific qubit severely restricts the connectivity between adjacent qubits, disrupts stabilizer measurements, induces frequent detection events, and consequently inflates the logical error rate. To mitigate this issue, various strategies have been proposed, such as syndrome extraction circuits based on stabilizer folding and the construction of super-stabilizers via gauge operators. However, these methods have primarily been developed for architectures with relatively high connectivity of 3 to 4. Furthermore, experimental demonstrations showing an actual reduction in logical error rates in the presence of physical hardware defects have been lacking. In this work, we propose a defect-avoidance scheme tailored for the heavy-hexagon architecture, which features a lower connectivity of 2 to 3. While previous approaches required four measurement cycles to measure all gauge operators comprising a super-stabilizer, our proposed scheme completes all gauge measurements within only two cycles. We experimentally validate this approach on IBM quantum hardware by identifying physical defects and implementing the defect-avoidance circuits. As a result, avoiding the defect yields an approximately 10\% reduction in the logical error rate per cycle for both logical $X$ and $Z$ errors. These findings demonstrate that defects in superconducting hardware can be successfully handled even under the constrained connectivity of heavy-hexagon lattices, and that defect avoidance substantially reduces logical errors on real quantum hardware.

**Authors:** Omri Farber, Gilad Gour

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14785v1

**Submitted:** 2026-10-10


### 摘要

### Abstract

Free classical communication can yield an unbounded single-shot saving in quantum compression, even though it leaves the asymptotic rate of an i.i.d. source unchanged. At every fixed error in $(0,1)$, we exhibit sources whose unassisted compression cost is arbitrarily large, yet a single qubit suffices with classical communication and no preshared entanglement. We establish this separation by determining the exact optimal source fidelity for compression through an $m$-dimensional noiseless quantum channel, with and without free classical communication, while preserving correlations with an inaccessible reference. Without classical assistance, the optimal fidelity is exactly the Ky Fan norm $\|ρ\|_{(m)}$, the sum of the $m$ largest eigenvalues of the source state. With classical assistance, we obtain an explicit spectral threshold formula and a protocol attaining it. Classical communication strictly improves fidelity whenever $m$ is smaller than the source rank. The corresponding compression costs are two smoothed versions of the log-rank entropy, also called max-entropy: trace-distance smoothing under local operations and quadratic smoothing under LOCC. For i.i.d. copies of a fixed source with positive entropy variance, however, the two costs have identical first-, second-, and third-order asymptotic terms. For nonlattice spectra, we determine the constant-order advantage: classical communication saves $\log_2(e)-1$ bits per block asymptotically, and the ratio of assisted to unassisted minimum communication dimensions converges to $2/e$.

### Abstract

Superradiance is a collective optical effect where emitters cooperatively radiate with a maximal intensity scaling proportional to the square of the number of emitters. Despite the superradiant cascade transitioning through highly entangled states, it has been shown that, for dynamics along the Dicke ladder, superradiance need not generate entanglement between the emitters. **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15199v1

**Submitted:** 2026-10-10


### 摘要

### Abstract

Area laws express the expectation that entanglement in gapped quantum ground states scales with a boundary rather than a volume. In one dimension this holds for every Rényi entropy, and the Rényi area-law conjecture asserts the same in higher dimensions for each fixed $0<α<1$. Here we disprove this conjecture in every spatial dimension two and higher. We construct finite-range Hamiltonians with fixed local dimension, bounded interaction strength, unique ground states and a uniform spectral gap, whose ground states exhibit volume-law Rényi entanglement while obeying a von Neumann area law and remaining connected to product states through uniformly gapped local paths. The mechanism is error-space complexity: locally identifiable components of arbitrarily small weight can encode states with high entanglement complexity. We also establish the worst-case rank-error trade-off for approximate ground-state projectors, a key tool in one-dimensional area-law proofs. At fixed gap, the optimal Schmidt rank at projection error $ε$ across a planar cut of cross-section $A$ scales as $\exp[\tildeΘ((A\logε^{-1})^{2/3})]$ when $\logε^{-1}\gtrsim A$. Thus $α=1$ is the sharp threshold for a universal Rényi area law within this adiabatically connected class.

### Abstract

Quantum computation offers a promising alternative to classical Monte Carlo methods by naturally avoiding sign problems through unitary evolution.
