### Abstract

We introduce IPQSSL, a quantum-informed regularized Poisson framework for graph-based semi-supervised classification under scarce labels. Local graph feature contexts are QR factorized, lifted to unitary encodings, and processed by a shared variational circuit whose measurements yield node wise diagonal weights that stabilize a symmetrized Poisson system. We establish positive definiteness of the symmetrized, regularized operator under explicit spectral conditions and derive a Hermitian, block encoding compatible formulation that separates the classical NISQ oriented implementation from a future fault-tolerant pathway. Across seven benchmarks spanning tabular data and a citation network, the effect of regularization is dataset- and budget-dependent: IPQSSL improves over the Poisson baselines on Iris and Wine at all but the single-label budget, reaching 0.946 accuracy on Iris at ten labels per class and at every budget on German Credit, where, however, all methods remain below the majority class rate. Original Poisson remains preferable on Breast Cancer, Heart Disease, Optdigits, and Cora, with the deficit narrowing as the label budget grows on the two largest graphs. Ablations attribute the gains to diagonal stabilization rather than variational training. The result is a resource transparent bridge between Poisson graph learning and quantum linear algebra, with an explicit empirical delineation of when diagonal regularization helps.

### Abstract

We study the average excess pseudo entropy, $ΔS_{12}$, in the Rice-Mele model under Thouless pumping. We compute it for two driving schemes, the control-freak and smooth pumps, under open and periodic boundary conditions. We find that $ΔS_{12}$ distinguishes the trivial, monomer, and topological stages of the pump, and that it depends on the boundary condition, the driving scheme, and the reference time. We also find that a nonzero staggered potential breaks chiral symmetry and gaps out the SSH critical point, thereby flattening the pseudo-entropy peak at the phase boundary. We also study reference-state dependence in the open Kitaev chain, comparing a fixed even-parity target with five reference families: even free-fermion, pairing-only, vacuum, and two fixed gapped states. We find that the pseudo-entropy response depends on the reference and on the parity of the subsystem size, even when the reference remains gapped. At $μ=0$, the pairing-only reference gives the exact result $S_{\mathrm{pr}}=\ln 2$ for $L=4m+2$, while the $L=4m$ sequence has analytically determined finite-size corrections. These results show that, in both models, pseudo entropy is a property of the reference-target pair and the subsystem partition, rather than a reference-independent signature of the topological phase.

**Authors:** Madhumita Sarkar, Sougato Bose, Roopayan Ghosh

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28215v1

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28215v1

**Submitted:** 2026-11-20


### 摘要

### Abstract

Dynamical freezing, the near-complete suppression of dynamics at special drive frequencies, offers a route to mitigating Floquet heating in periodically driven quantum systems. In interacting systems, however, the energy cost of each transition depends on the many-body configuration, so that the freezing condition itself becomes state dependent. We study this dependence in periodically driven Rydberg atom chains under single- and bi-frequency driving. Using Floquet perturbation theory, we show that each spin flip is characterised by a single interference amplitude set by its local interaction shift, and that a state freezes only where the amplitudes of all its available flips vanish simultaneously. This picture accounts quantitatively for the shift and proliferation of freezing frequencies with interatomic spacing and for the response of entire families of initial states, which are sorted by a single local motif. Freezing is strong only within a window of spacings bounded by interaction tails on one side and nearest-neighbour excitation facilitation on the other, outside of which the advantage of bi-frequency driving is lost. In the regime of large detuning amplitude, where freezing points are numerous and the perturbative description is controlled, this window leaves almost no room for the PXP limit.

### Abstract

Skip-gate leakage --- such as atom loss in neutral-atom arrays or trapped ions, or photon loss in superconducting dual-rail qubits --- takes a qubit out of its computational subspace and removes subsequent gates acting on it. If leakage is only detectable end-of-the-line at qubit measurement, error correction circuits must be designed to periodically measure all qubits, as in SWAP-based circuits and foliation. We show that the best SWAP-based surface code, although optimal against space-like skip-gate leakage errors, is suboptimal against time-like leakage errors. By optimal, we mean that the code corrects as many leakage errors as it can detect. Remarkably, we find that the foliated surface code is optimal against both space- and time-like error configurations. We support our findings with numerical simulations. Under two-qubit gate leakage with probability 0.5%, foliation suppresses time-like errors in a distance-4 rotated stability experiment by 1380(50) per two added rounds, compared to 63(1) for the best SWAP-based circuit. In memory, the two are comparable. Under an experimentally motivated neutral-atom noise model, we find that foliation outperforms SWAP-based circuits in stability for both rotated and unrotated code geometries. This holds true for a parameter sweep that takes the neutral-atom noise model from being dominated by computational errors to atom loss. Interestingly, a SWAP-based circuit for unrotated code memory performs best when computational noise dominates over loss. Our work motivates co-designing error correction circuits with logical operations: preferring foliation when time-like errors dominate and SWAP-based circuits when space-like errors dominate.

### Abstract

Learning-based quantum error mitigation infers noise-free results from noisy measurement data. However, exact training labels become prohibitively expensive to compute as circuits grow, and the generalization capability of a trained model to infer accurate results on circuits of depths and sizes it was not trained on poses a challenge. We show that circuit cutting allows such a model to be reused on circuits larger than those it was trained on. We benchmark this approach against zero-noise extrapolation, Clifford data regression, and probabilistic error cancellation on simulated circuits several times wider and deeper than the training circuits. On random-circuit chains, the fragment-wise mitigator removes most of the reconstructed error. For a holographic Ising-chain eigensolver under a fixed shot budget, it recovers most of the noise-induced energy error, close to 90\% or more, and outperforms Clifford data regression and probabilistic error cancellation. Since the mitigator is trained once and needs no additional circuits or noise amplification at inference, its cost is paid up front rather than for every new circuit. The approach is also modular. Any improvement to the small-circuit mitigator, such as retraining on larger fragments or on device-specific noise, carries over directly to every circuit that can be cut into such fragments. Our proposal thus offers a practical route to bringing learned error mitigation to quantum computations far beyond the reach of exact training labels, provided they can be partitioned with a moderate number of cuts.

### Abstract

Algorithmic primitives such as adders, multipliers, and Boolean logic typically contain clusters of multi-qubit non-Clifford gates, whose decomposition into $T$ and $CCZ$ gates adds scheduling and spacetime overhead in fault-tolerant quantum computers. For a small classical system correlated with a large one, it is always possible to compress the large system down to a size only dependent on the size of the smaller one, while preserving all correlations up to a given controllable error.
