### Abstract

Ordinary $0$-form symmetries $G$ can mix with $1$-form symmetries $Π_2$ to form a $2$-group $\mathcal{G}$. We give a concrete lattice definition for a class of finite $2$-group symmetries in terms of local relations among symmetry operators. These relations identify the Postnikov class $[ρ]\in H^3(BG,Π_2)$ that characterizes the mixing. We develop a method to extract anomalies of these symmetries in one spatial dimension by restricting symmetry operations to an interval and comparing their composition near its endpoints. We also construct explicit qubit symmetry actions in $d=1,2,3$ spatial dimensions for a $2$-group with $G=Π_2=\mathbb{Z}_2$. These actions share the same nontrivial Postnikov class but include both anomaly-free and anomalous realizations. Their cohomological anomalies are classified by $H^{d+2}(B\mathcal G,U(1))$, which is $\mathbb{Z}_2$ for $d=1,2$ and $\mathbb{Z}_2\times\mathbb{Z}_2$ for $d=3$, and our constructions realize every class in these dimensions. Our results connect higher-group symmetry and its anomalies to concrete lattice operations, providing a microscopic framework for studying anomalous symmetries.

### Abstract

Preparing complex quantum states of light for quantum information processing tasks often proceeds by converting from simpler states via experimentally accessible operations. We provide fundamental bounds on such probabilistic conversions, both exact and approximate. To do so, we establish a theory of weak, fully relative majorization that compares the disorder of phase-space functions. ### Abstract

We introduce two-dimensional toric code models with additional long-range loop interactions. ### Abstract

Grover's algorithm is a textbook example of quantum advantage, providing a quadratic query speedup over classical methods for unstructured search. In this Letter, we characterize the non-stabilizerness, or magic, developed throughout Grover's protocol, quantifying quantum complexity with respect to the efficiently simulable stabilizer states. First, we compute the compilation-independent magic at the end of each Grover iteration, deriving an analytic prediction for the stabilizer Rényi entropy and proving that it remains intensive as the system size grows.
