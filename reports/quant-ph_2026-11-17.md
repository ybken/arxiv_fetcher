### Abstract

An optimized commutator bound can lie strictly below the quantum infimum even when its minimizing mean is realized by a quantum state. We study hybrid uncertainty, defined as the sum of pairwise products of standard deviations. For any spanning list of nonzero real linear combinations of the generators in a positive discrete series representation of $\mathfrak{su}(1,1)$, the commutator bound has a unique minimizing mean, realized by a Perelomov coherent state. We prove that the optimized bound equals the infimum over states with finite second moments if and only if the symmetrized covariance matrix of the prescribed observables is diagonal in this state. When this condition fails, the quantum infimum is strictly greater than the bound; for a nonorthogonal list, we obtain an explicit positive lower bound on this gap. For irreducible spin-$j$ representations of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$, we derive the exact minimum of the spectral hybrid uncertainty over states for any spanning list of spin observables. For a fixed state, we compare separate measurements made before a random target is disclosed with direct measurement of the target known in advance. For these protocols, the excess mean squared error coefficient equals twice the listed hybrid uncertainty, and its minimum over orthogonal combinations of the observables equals twice the spectral hybrid uncertainty.

### Abstract

By dropping the idea that completeness is a mandatory prerequisite for agents' rationality, this work shows how classical and quantum subjective probability attributions represent suitable preference orderings. To do so, we develop an extension of the Anscombe--Aumann framework in which acts map a finite set of states of the world into the effect interval of a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, while preferences need not be complete. The construction keeps three logically distinct questions separate. First, an incomplete-preference multi-utility theorem supplies affine utility functionals. Second, finite-dimensional operator geometry determines the form of each affine functional. Third, admissibility identifies which normalised probability--state pairs are compatible with the full preference. We introduce behavioural state-independence as the natural Anscombe--Aumann interpretation of the idea that the identity of the states of the world should not affect how quantum consequences are compared. Because an incomplete preference makes this aggregate behavioural condition weaker than a utility-by-utility condition, the factorisation theorem requires a stronger, explicitly operational representation-level condition. Under that condition and Loewner monotonicity, every affine utility functional is positively affinely equivalent to a normalised utility of the form $ U_{p,ρ} (f)= \sum_{s} p(s) Tr( ρf(s) )$. Consequently, a suitable incomplete preference relation is represented by a family of admissible pairs in $Δ(S)\times D(H)$. We then identify a canonical maximal compatible family and show that the scalar-effect subspace carries an ordinary classical multi-utility representation, with scalar events appearing as the vertices of the scalar-act cube.

### Abstract

Cooling to ground states of many-body Hamiltonians by imaginary-time evolution is central to quantum simulation, but its non-unitary aspect prevents direct implementation on a quantum computer. Existing routes restore it by importing structure from outside the intrinsic quantum dynamics, e.g. by parameter estimation on many copies or recursive compilation. Here, we show that a single randomized snapshot of the quantum many-body system is sufficient to steer the state along the cooling direction on average. Our algorithm, shadow chain, uses randomized measurements to collapse the state to its classical record, which fixes the next unitary update. Snapshots across steps form a trajectory that is a quantum-executable sample of an auxiliary-field path integral; fluctuations among trajectories are exponentially suppressed in system size. With the randomness supplied intrinsically by the Born measurement rule, the trajectories sample themselves rather than going through external accept-reject steps. The resulting algorithm is ansatz-free and exact upon extrapolation to the continuous limit, with the exponential cost confined only to system size rather than temperature.

### Abstract

A major challenge in quantum computing is to systematically check if a quantum program implements its desired functionality. For classical programs, testing is widely used; each test case comprises a pair of sample input and expected output to be compared with the actual output. To increase the number of test cases, however, one has to solve the test-oracle problem: for each additional test input one has to know the correct output. Metamorphic testing tackles this problem by utilizing metamorphic relations between input and output domains to automatically derive new test cases from existing ones. However, adapting metamorphic testing to the quantum realm is not straightforward, since quantum states cannot be directly inspected. We propose a framework of quantum metamorphic testing (QMT) in which the metamorphic relations incorporate the measurement procedures required to assess quantum outputs. We apply our QMT framework to testing the preparation of stabilizer states, which are central to protocols like measurement-based quantum computing or quantum error correction. We utilize structural properties of stabilizer states to systematically derive measurement circuits for new test cases from existing ones. We implement this framework in Qiskit, and demonstrate that it reduces by an order of magnitude the testing run time compared to testing with randomly generated input states. We further show effectiveness by revealing synthetic program faults injected by circuit mutations, as well as a real bug in the Qiskit compiler.
