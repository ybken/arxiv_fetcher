**Submitted:** 2026-11-17

### Abstract

The Schrieffer-Wolff transformation (SWT) is a cornerstone of effective-Hamiltonian theory. However, the SWT is predominantly used for weak-coupling problems, while a systematic counterpart for strongly coupled regimes remains largely unexplored. A general closed-form SWT generator and a clear physical principle for gauge fixing remain elusive, obscuring the transformation's underlying structure. We introduce the dual SWT to deal with the strong-coupling problems. The generalized SWT (gSWT) is formulated for any Hermitian operator and block partition. We establish the geodesic principle for its canonical gauge. Furthermore, we derive an exact closed-form solution of the gSWT generator at every order as a finite nested-commutator sum whose only input is the active gap set. Both the geometric and algebraic aspects of the SWT physics are revealed. The gSWT framework is illustrated in two quantum many-body models, the collective spin model and the transverse-field Ising model.

### Abstract

Classical sampling of quantum error correction (QEC) circuits supports fault-tolerant protocol evaluation, but estimating rare logical failures requires many independent shots. Frame-active-state simulators isolate non-stabilizer information in a dense active-state vector, yet dense-state processing remains exponential in the active width. This paper presents MASQ, an exact measurement-adaptive sampler for near-Clifford QEC circuits based on selective materialization of non-Clifford rotations. At each measurement boundary, MASQ materializes only rotations in a measurement-relevant closure capturing direct and indirect non-commuting dependencies, deferring the rest to limit active-state expansion. Stabilizer-assisted elimination further removes rotations certified to be invisible to all future measurements. A circuit-dependent schedule is computed once and shared across shots, while adaptive caching of conditional Born probabilities and batched execution reduce repeated sampling work while preserving the measurement-record distribution. Across 19 QEC benchmarks, MASQ reduces peak active width by up to 40 active virtual qubits and achieves up to 8,098-fold CPU throughput speedup over Clifft on multi-round circuits.

### Abstract

Hardware quantum error correction (QEC) decoders require validation of logical error suppression and timing compliance before deployment. Rare logical failures and deadline violations demand extensive sampling, while algorithm-level software evaluation does not directly measure hardware response times. Circuit-level noise models contain many error mechanisms but few firings per shot at low physical error rates, making exhaustive per-shot scanning inefficient. We present CHEQ, an FPGA platform integrating on-chip sampling, concurrent decoder evaluation, scoring, and RISC-V control. Its Sampling Units use geometric skipping to sample firing locations from detector error models and amortize mechanism traversal across shots within each batch. ### Abstract

Accurate magnetic-field evolution of spectra requires a common wavefunction representation that consistently describes multiple states across different field regimes. Discrete Variable Representation (DVR) methods combine accurate basis-based kinetic-energy evaluation with flexible real-space potential representation, enabling arbitrary non-analytic potentials to be treated. Here, we develop a magnetic-field-dependent Hermite function-based (Hermite-B) DVR that adapts naturally to magnetic confinement, efficiently representing compressed wavefunctions across a wide range of field strengths while remaining centered in regions relevant to the scalar confinement potential. This ensures accurate treatment of the field-dependent kinetic energy while addressing two key challenges: maintaining accuracy from the Zeeman to Landau regimes and accommodating diverse confinement potentials. We also introduce a spatial restriction scheme that confines Hermite DVR grid points to a fixed-length domain, enabling compact representations for smooth potentials. The method is benchmarked for two-dimensional quantum dots in perpendicular magnetic fields against analytical models and compared with finite-grid, sine DVR, and field-free Hermite DVR calculations. The performance of field-independent DVR methods for magnetically confined systems is also investigated for the first time. Excellent agreement with exact analytical spectra and wavefunctions is obtained, with Hermite-B requiring the fewest basis functions and smaller Hamiltonian matrices, improving computational efficiency. Applications to arbitrary confinement potentials and tilted magnetic fields demonstrate its versatility beyond analytically solvable systems. The proposed method offers a robust, accurate, and efficient framework for large-scale calculations of realistic semiconductor nanostructures and molecular systems in external magnetic fields.

### Abstract

How much can be prepared with a quantum system whose Hamiltonian cannot be modulated in time? Common intuition suggests that the answer is not much. Without a knob to turn during the evolution, a system seems confined to a fixed trajectory through its state space, while time-dependent control can steer it almost anywhere. We put this intuition to the test by studying autonomous control, i.e., state preparation with a time-invariant Hamiltonian, where the only freedom lies in the static coupling strengths and the evolution time. We characterize basic mathematical properties of the autonomously reachable set and introduce two criteria of unreachability: a certificate that is cheap to evaluate and rigorously excludes that a target state can be reached for any choice of control parameters, and a stronger criterion based on exhaustive numerical optimization. With these tools we estimate the probability that a typical state in Hilbert space is unreachable under randomly drawn control terms. The dimensionality of the reachable set turns out to be independent of the system, yet the fraction of Hilbert space lying close to it depends strongly on the choice of control Hamiltonians. Strikingly, for sufficiently random controls and long enough evolution times, almost every state can become approximately reachable, even with the fewest generators that permit full controllability with time-dependent couplings. Autonomous control can approach the expressivity of time-dependent control, at the cost of longer evolution times.

### Abstract

Ground-state energy estimation (GSEE) and ground-state preparation (GSP) are fundamental problems in quantum computing. Given a Hamiltonian $H$ with $\|H\|\le1$ and a guiding state $|ψ\rangle$ with overlap at least $γ>0$ with the ground space of $H$, the goal in GSEE is to estimate the ground-state energy of $H$ to additive error $δ$ with failure probability at most $\varepsilon$, while in GSP it is to prepare a state with ground-space weight at least $1-\varepsilon$, given a gap lower bound $Δ$. We also show that these constructions recover the earlier time-evolution and block-encoding lower-bound scalings. Thus, we obtain $Θ(\sqrt{s}\log(1/\varepsilon)/(γδ))$ for GSEE and $Θ(\sqrt{s}\log(1/\varepsilon)/(γΔ))$ for GSP, showing that $\sqrt{s}$ is necessary and sufficient and completing the joint dependence on all problem parameters.
