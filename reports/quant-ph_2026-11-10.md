### Abstract

Krylov subspace methods efficiently approximate extremal eigenstates and eigenvalues of many-body Hamiltonians, but non-orthogonal bases can suffer from ill-conditioning. This challenge is amplified in quantum computing, where direct state access and thus straightforward orthogonalization are unavailable. We investigate a variational Krylov-like framework that iteratively optimizes a parametrized sequence of states to mimic Lanczos-like couplings while penalizing linear dependencies. Analyzed against exact Krylov and Lanczos on a finite Anderson impurity model, ansatz expressivity strongly influences the performance: Hamiltonian-inspired ansätze preserve Krylov-like convergence while improving the conditioning, whereas shallow ansätze remain stable but miss low-energy directions.

### Abstract

How a quantum resource can be quantified depends on which operations are free. Yet two proposed quantifiers of quantum state texture can increase under free operations: the Wigner-Yanase-Dyson skew information relative to the unique textureless state and the squared Hilbert-Schmidt distance from that state. We show reachability from every state orthogonal to the textureless state, whether pure or mixed: each can be freely transformed into any other state. Therefore, every texture monotone must assign the same maximal value to all such states, a requirement that neither candidate satisfies. We then construct valid texture monotones by adding the population outside the textureless state to the skew information and by maximizing the squared Hilbert-Schmidt distance over free operations. We prove faithfulness, convexity, and invariance under textureless ancillas, characterize additivity properties and strong monotonicity, satisfied only in the Wigner-Yanase case, and derive tight bounds for both monotones at fixed overlap with the textureless state and characterize all saturating states. Together, these results show how allowed state transformations impose operational constraints on the quantification of quantum state texture.

Every PVM-critical entangled two-qubit state becomes steerable with two copies. We prove this for the full critical boundary and for two different critical inputs. ### Abstract

Moment-based tomography efficiently reconstructs Gaussian states, but stochastic phase noise generally produces mixed, non-Gaussian outputs. ### Abstract

We investigate the spectroscopic response of a repulsively interacting, infinitely heavy impurity moving at constant velocity $v$ through a one-dimensional ideal Fermi gas. ### Abstract
