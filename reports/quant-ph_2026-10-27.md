### Abstract

The continued growth of orbital debris threatens the safety and sustainability of near-Earth space, making Active Debris Removal (ADR) an important mitigation strategy. Selecting $k$ targets from $N$ candidates yields a combinatorial search space of size $\binom{N}{k}$, making joint target selection and mission sequencing computationally demanding. We present a topology-aware quantum-classical framework integrating orbital mechanics, weighted orbital-transfer graphs, Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS), and multi-objective evolutionary optimization. Seven target-prioritization strategies represent physical consequence, orbital persistence, conjunction risk, network centrality, community structure, inferred source families, and random selection. GBS generates candidate target subsets from the weighted graph, which are then evaluated using PyGMO-based evolutionary optimization to identify servicing sequences that jointly minimize total velocity increment ($Δv$) and mission duration, accounting for modeled timing, propulsion, and mass feasibility. We evaluate the framework across three benchmark environments, SOCRATES Minefield, Collision Cloud, and Random, with candidate-pool sizes $N\in\{15,18,22,25\}$ and target cardinalities $k\in\{3,5\}$. The experiments characterize how benchmark topology, prioritization strategy, and problem scale relate to sampling behavior and downstream mission performance. By separating candidate-subset generation from detailed trajectory optimization, the framework offers a topology-aware approach to focusing computational effort on a limited set of candidate ADR missions.

### Abstract

We present the resource estimation of how known-constant arithmetic changes the cost of a Grover oracle for Minecraft 1.13.1 seed recovery, an application of quantum search to procedural content generation. The oracle developed here combines a structure-placement predicate with a reversible inverse transform of the linear congruential generator for the 32-bit residual search. Reduced-width arithmetic, phase marking, uncomputation, and complete Grover loops are verified against an independent Java reference, while production-width circuits are structurally audited. Direct Fourier-basis constant arithmetic reduces the placement-predicate width from 6n+3 to 4n+3 qubits and the structural scaling of the two-qubit gate counts from Theta(n^3) to O(n^2). Under backend-agnostic common-basis Qiskit level-0 {u, CX} accounting, the representative nonzero-r folded residual-search construction uses 275 circuit qubits, 48,896 CX gates, and depth 4,482, versus 467 qubits, 2,038,804 CX gates, and depth 186,328 for the original register-to-register baseline. Multi-placement conjunctions expose a width-recomputation tradeoff, and exact enumeration identifies a plateau in candidate reduction for the benchmark. We executed reduced-width placement circuits within complete Grover loops on IBM processors and Quantinuum H2-1 and show larger one-iteration increases in marked-state probability for the specialized construction than for the baseline. These results quantify the resource benefits of arithmetic specialization and show substantial routing sensitivity in the reduced-width IBM hardware experiments. Together, the results quantify the effect of specializing known-constant arithmetic within a PCG-derived oracle and show that its compiled hardware behavior strongly depends on routing and execution context.

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33144v1

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33144v1

**Submitted:** 2026-10-23

### Abstract

Parametric quantum circuits are usually trained by classical, local optimizers, which use the quantum computer only to evaluate the cost function, or its gradient, one point at a time. Quantum Hamiltonian Descent (QHD) takes a different route: in this quantum algorithm for continuous optimization, the parameters become the position of a quantum particle, with the cost function as its potential, and two time schedules control kinetic and potential energy so that the wavefunction explores the whole landscape and tunnels towards the global minimum. QHD, however, assumes a classically computable cost function, and its digital implementation is poorly understood: standard error bounds grow exponentially with the number of qubits per parameter, and the schedules are borrowed from classical optimization. Here we propose to train parametric circuits with QHD entirely on the quantum computer, through an online step in which the qubits encoding the parameters coherently control the circuit on a second register. We prove that, for cost functions with a smooth periodic extension, including those of parametric circuits, the simulation error admits a bound independent of the grid resolution, and that for circuits the bound grows at most linearly with the number of parameters. We also show that the ideal dynamics depends on the two schedules only through a single curve, which controls the adiabaticity of the search, and that the remaining freedom can be used to minimize the gate count. Simulations on benchmark functions and parametric circuits show that, at equal cost, schedules designed in this way markedly increase the probability of finding the global minimum.

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33160v1

### Abstract

Links of rings offer an intuitive picture of multipartite entanglement. Cutting one ring can free some of the others, just as losing a particle, i.e. tracing it out, can disentangle some of the remaining ones. This analogy classifies multipartite entanglement according to which groups of particles stay entangled when others are lost, a question at the heart of robust quantum networks. However, pure states realizing a given link class were known only in special cases. Here, we close this gap. Recasting link polynomials as hypergraphs, we give an explicit formula for pure qudit states and prove that, for every link class, their entanglement under any pattern of particle loss matches the link exactly. The same construction also controls genuine multipartite entanglement, determining exactly which of the groups that stay entangled are genuinely multipartite entangled, and all of them can be made so. As a consequence, we prove the conjecture that $m$-resistant pure qudit states, which stay entangled after the loss of any $m$ particles but become fully separable after the loss of $m+1$, exist for any number of particles and any $m$.

### Abstract

Periodic driving provides a powerful route for controlling localization and engineering effective Hamiltonians in quantum systems. A localized one-dimensional Aubry-André chain can be driven into a fully delocalized phase when the entire system is driven below a critical frequency. Here, we ask whether such delocalization can be achieved by driving only a part of the system. Our results demonstrate an alternative route to Floquet engineering, in which the transport properties of a quantum lattice system can be modified by driving only a subsystem.
