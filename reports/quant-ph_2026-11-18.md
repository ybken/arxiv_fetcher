**Authors:** Martin Janßen

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21681v1

**Submitted:** 2026-11-15

### Abstract

For a time $t_{c}$ of calculation in an $N$ qubit quantum computer that is much shorter than the intrinsic Heisenberg time $t_H$, $t_c\ll t_H$, the dynamics of the system is effectively irreversible and the couplings between the qubits give rise to a relaxation time scale $τ_r$. If $t_H\gg t_c >\sim τ_r$ one looses quantum coherence exponentially fast. Since $t_H$ scales exponentially with $N$ there is a threshold $N_c$ such that for $N\gg N_c$ one must be aware of possible intrinsic relaxation during calculation. As an order of magnitude estimation we find $N_c\approx \ln(τ_r/T)$ where $T$ is a typical system specific oscillation time of a single qubit. $τ_r$ can be estimated as the typical inverse transition rate between two parts of the system, one of which is treated as an environment of the other.

### Abstract

In this work, we propose a novel approach to handling the independent and identically distributed assumption for the state preparation in prepare-and measure QKD protocols. Contrary to previous approaches, we do not prove the security using sub-protocols, and instead opt to guarantee security in the presence of correlations by dividing the communication into finite blocks of consecutive pulses, and adding an additional assumption of no dependence between each block. ### Abstract

We construct and solve the three-colored Kitaev model on finite hyperbolic lattices with periodic boundary conditions. We describe a self-contained procedure which uses finite quotients of orientation- preserving triangle groups to generate regular maps on compact genus-g surfaces. From these, one can extract their lattice connectivity, plaquette structure, homology cycles and Wilson sectors while implementing consistent boundary conditions. On the example of {8, 3} and {12, 3} lattices, we enumerate a selection of quotients with up to 72 vertices, which constitute the discrete steps in a finite-size scaling analysis. For the isotropic point, corresponding to the spin-liquid phase in the thermodynamic limit, we identify the ground state superselection sectors, quantify their deviation from the homogeneous π flux sector and show how the selected flux background depends on the quotient geometry. We then discuss how the resulting ground state sector degeneracies are entirely generated by physical lattice symmetries acting on both the plaquette fluxes and non-contractible Wilson loops. Finally, we study the behaviour of the finite-size two-vison gap and matter fermion correlations. We find that the gap does not decrease monotonically with increasing system size, while one still recovers the expected algebraic decay of the correlations at the isotropic point, even for the considered modest system sizes.

**Submitted:** 2026-11-16

### Abstract

Quantum advantage encompasses fundamentally different claims, from resource reductions to exponential separations in sample complexity. Evaluating these claims requires distinguishing how an algorithm achieves an advantage from what is necessary for the separation and how strongly it is supported against classical alternatives. We propose a multi-label taxonomy that makes these distinctions explicit, classifying structural, interference, and coherent mechanisms while separately recording their necessity and the evidence for the classical comparison. Coherent mechanisms are further divided into memory and output, reflecting distinct restrictions on quantum information processing. A two-part entanglement-breaking test distinguishes loss of information from loss of algorithmic efficiency. The hidden subgroup problem illustrates the distinction: among the cases examined, cross-copy coherence is information-theoretically necessary only for the symmetric group. We present this as a diagnostic methodology rather than a theory of quantum advantage: for a new claim, it identifies the relevant adversary, separates the mechanisms a protocol uses from those shown to be necessary, and records the strength of the evidence and the quality of the advantage separately.

**Authors:** Jong Yeon Lee

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21709v1

**Submitted:** 2026-11-16


### 摘要

### Abstract

We present QEDist, a method and software package that certifies the distance of qubit stabilizer codes. Building on tree-search methods, QEDist constructs candidate logical operators one qubit at a time, using sparse parity checks, stabilizer equivalence, and verified symmetries to reduce the work. For a cutoff $W$, a completed search either finds a nontrivial logical operator of weight at most $W$ or proves that none exists. Because satisfying one check can violate another, we use both satisfied and unsatisfied checks to lower-bound the weight needed to complete a logical operator, and prune branches whose bound exceeds the remaining budget, which substantially reduces net search time. We benchmark QEDist mainly on Calderbank-Shor-Steane (CSS) codes; the same formulation extends to non-CSS codes and to the fault distance of syndrome-extraction circuits. For lifted-product (LP) codes, we further exploit the product structure to replace part of the full-code search with a smaller search on a classical constituent. We establish exact distances for codes with up to several thousand qubits, including Abelian and non-Abelian LP codes whose distances were previously reported only as upper bounds. In several seconds of search on one laptop core, QEDist certifies exact distances up to 20 for many of these codes and fault distance 10 for the syndrome-extraction circuit of the $[[144,12,12]]$ code. With orders-of-magnitude speedups over the tested exact solvers, QEDist makes exact distance certification a practical tool for selecting and optimizing finite-length quantum codes.
