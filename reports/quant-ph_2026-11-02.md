**Authors:** Zhuocheng Ma, Hongzheng Zhao, Liang-You Peng

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39169v2

**Submitted:** 2026-10-29

### Abstract

Static coupling to a dissipative cavity has been proposed as a route to autonomously stabilize Floquet eigenstates. The cavity provides an energy-selective relaxation channel: when its photon energy matches a quasienergy difference modulo drive quanta, it mediates transitions between Floquet states, followed by irreversible photon loss. Here, we show that at intermediate driving frequencies, this resonance condition alone is not sufficient for stabilization. Instead, the steady state is controlled by the interplay between resonance selection and the cavity-induced transition strengths between Floquet states, which can either cooperate or compete. This interplay leads to successful stabilization, mixed steady states despite resonance, and complete breakdown near Floquet-zone hybridization. We further show that the same mechanism extends to multilevel spin-S systems, providing a general framework for understanding when static dissipation can reliably stabilize driven quantum states.

**Submitted:** 2026-10-29

### Abstract

Cross-platform verification compares output states of independently operated bosonic and quantum optical systems when classical predictions and joint measurements are unavailable. Continuous-variable distributed inner product estimation (CV-DIPE) provides a local measurement approach, but infinite-dimensional state spaces require rigorous control of truncation error. We develop three CV-DIPE protocols for the natural class of energy-constrained continuous-variable quantum states. One uses coordinated displaced parity, another coordinated Haar measurements, and the third independent homodyne shadows. For arbitrary multimode inputs in this class, including entangled and non-Gaussian states, we establish truncation error bounds and worst-case sample complexity guarantees for the exact inner product. The leading terms of these bounds favor coordinated Haar measurements, whose global implementation is experimentally challenging. These protocols provide certified strategies for cross-platform verification of continuous-variable systems using only local measurement data.

### Abstract

### Abstract

Quantum synchronization holds promise in advancing quantum technologies such as sensing, communication, and computation. A key challenge in this rapidly developing field is to identify mechanisms that can promote the emergence of synchronous dynamics. Here, we show that quantum synchronization can be enhanced by introducing controlled heterogeneity in the form of coupling mismatches. ### Abstract

Driven-dissipative quantum devices are routinely operated with time-dependent drives and exposed to temporally correlated classical noise. When these perturbations vary on timescales comparable to the system's intrinsic dynamics, rotating-wave and Markovian approximations can fail, making effective static descriptions unreliable. As a result, accurately modeling systems in this regime often relies on simulating the full time-dependent master equation. This is particularly demanding for processes requiring long integration times, such as rare switching between metastable states, or for stochastic perturbations requiring averaging over many noise realizations. To address these challenges, we introduce Liouvillian spectral snapshots (LSS), a reduced-basis method that enables efficient simulation of such systems by projecting the master equation onto a low-dimensional subspace capturing their coarse-grained dynamics. LSS constructs this subspace by computing the slowly decaying, low-frequency eigenoperators of the Liouvillian at a few adaptively sampled perturbation values and compressing them into a common basis. This basis is built once and can be reused across noise realizations and perturbation parameters, reducing the cost of ensemble averages and parameter sweeps.

### Abstract
