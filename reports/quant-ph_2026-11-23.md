### Abstract

Chiral (or sublattice) symmetry describes bipartite systems such as the Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain where same-sublattice A-A and B-B hoppings are forbidden, enabling the protection of topological phases. Here we identify new topological phases protected by half-chiral symmetry wherein chiral symmetry is preserved on only one sublattice, as may occur with sublattice imbalance (e.g. graphene-hBN) or localized orbitals (e.g. zincblende). Unlike conventional symmetry operators described by group theory, half-chiral symmetry is an algebraic constraint on the sublattice-projected Hamiltonian such as $PHP=0$, requiring a new framework for topological protection. In two dimensions we construct a half-chiral Kekule honeycomb model and show that its unusual higher-order topology protects a single zero-energy mode at a single corner.

### Abstract

Gauge theories constitute the basis of our microscopic description of fundamental physical laws, and they are a cornerstone of contemporary scientific research. Yet, how such systems thermalize far from equilibrium remains a fundamental open question at the crossroads of statistical, high-energy, and condensed-matter physics. In this work, we investigate the thermalization properties of non-Abelian lattice gauge theories, focusing on the discrete gauge group $D_3$, which is ideally suited for current qudit-based quantum simulators. Specifically, we study a linear plaquette chain in a gauge-fixed formulation that removes unphysical degrees of freedom. A numerical analysis of the Hamiltonian spectrum and expectation values of local operators shows a behavior broadly aligned with the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH). We also study thermalization through the real-time dynamics of the entanglement spectrum, confirming the emergence of random-matrix level statistics after an initial transient. Our results expand the rich phenomenology of dihedral non-Abelian gauge systems. Once the relevant symmetry structure is properly resolved, the model displays the same qualitative ETH-based thermalization scenario observed in Abelian gauge models and generic non-integrable quantum many-body systems.

### Abstract

Recent works have proposed semi-device-independent frameworks to certify non-stabilizerness, but the complexity of the stabilizer polytope makes these methods infeasible beyond a few qubits. In this work, we address this limitation by moving to partially characterized measurements in the context of worst-case random access codes. We first establish a simple condition, which we term the stabilizer pigeonhole criterion, that certifies non-stabilizerness in $m$-qubit pure states, and show how it can be applied to reduced states. As an application, we certify magic in $W$ states of arbitrary size using only eight preparations and three single-qubit measurement settings. Motivated by recent work on the stabilizer marginal problem, we then construct prepare-and-measure correlations generated by global $m$-qubit quantum states for which every $k$-qubit marginal lies inside the stabilizer polytope, yet the combined correlations cannot be reproduced by any global stabilizer mixture. Finally, we derive analytical upper and lower bounds on the worst-case bias attainable with mixed stabilizer preparations in an $m$-qubit random access code with $n$ Pauli measurements. For the complete set of $4^m-1$ non-identity Pauli measurements, we show that the optimal quantum and mixed-stabilizer biases both scale as $2^{-3m/2}$, and that their ratio is asymptotically bounded by a constant ($\lesssim 1.85$), showing that in this setting, magic in the preparations alone yields at most a constant-factor advantage over stabilizer mixtures.

### Abstract

Simulating lattice gauge theories on a digital quantum computer or using tensor network methods requires a finite-dimensional approximation of the gauge field, a process often called digitization. Controlling the resulting error is essential for assessing the accuracy and resource requirements of these simulations. In this work, we derive a digitization error bound for low-lying eigenstates of the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian for pure U(1) gauge theory and translate it into a bound on time-evolution error. ### Abstract

If the Hamiltonian of a large quantum system depends on a variable parameter, its parameter-dependent energy spectrum may often form a dense lattice of avoided crossings. If the parameter slowly changes, the system's time evolution through the avoided crossings tends in the semi-classical limit to change from adiabatic to diabatic across a narrow energy range that has recently been defined as a quantum separatrix. Here we show that in the extreme semi-classical limit of a quasi-continuously dense spectrum, all quantum separatrices form a universal one-parameter family, and that evolution through the separatrix becomes efficiently ergodizing. If the unitary evolution is realistically averaged over small variations in the parameter time dependence, quantum separatrix crossing generates diagonal entropy according to a universal formula. Numerical examples confirm that the results for quasi-continuous spectra remain clearly recognizable in finite systems, with only small finite-size effects.

### Abstract

Gravity-induced entanglement provides an operational test of whether gravitational dynamics are compatible with local operations and classical communication (LOCC), but the weakness of the gravitational interaction and the presence of force noise make direct entanglement generation extremely demanding. We introduce a controllable additional coherent interaction that biases two test masses close to, but within, a separability-preservation bound, so that the weak Newtonian coupling supplies only the remaining coherent contribution needed for entanglement. ### Abstract

The awkward disconnect in the postulates of quantum mechanics between the continuous, deterministic, linear, and unitary evolution governed by Schrödinger's equation and the discontinuous, stochastic, nonlinear, and nonunitary measurement process has vexed physicists for decades. Intuition from classical mechanics, statistical mechanics, and dynamical systems theory suggests that such a process might be modeled with a nonlinear system of differential equations. We explore such approaches by designing a deterministic model of an ideal projective measurement on a single qubit and constructing an analog computer that simulates it by manipulating voltages in an electrical circuit.
