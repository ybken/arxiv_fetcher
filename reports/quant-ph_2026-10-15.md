### Abstract

Optical lattice clocks in space could improve satellite navigation systems and enable new tests of fundamental physics, including gravitational-wave detection and tests of relativity. In microgravity, atoms can tunnel to degenerate neighboring lattice sites, broadening the clock transition and limiting coherence times. We demonstrate Aubry-André localization in a bichromatic optical lattice formed by two magic-wavelength lasers, suppressing tunneling in a horizontal lattice clock emulating free fall. We recover a Fourier-limited clock Rabi spectrum and measure Ramsey coherence times of up to 2 s in shallow lattices, with up to 100-fold improvement over the delocalized regime. These results establish Aubry-André localization as a practical route to controlling tunneling in future space-based clocks, and open a path to operating terrestrial clocks at shallower lattice depths.

**Authors:** Sander Stammbach, Giulio Gasbarri, Stefan Nimmrichter

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18237v2

**Submitted:** 2026-10-13

### Abstract

We present a fully autonomous model of a mechanical Maxwell demon engine that converts heat absorbed by a qubit into population inversion in an attached quantum battery. A damped mechanical oscillator acts as a pointer that incorporates the essential demon function of information acquisition about the qubit state and subsequent feedback into the system dynamics, replacing external measurements and control operations. This is achieved by means of a Holstein-type Hamiltonian, which makes the pointer's equilibrium position depend on the qubit state and thereby induces a state-dependent resonance shift. The mechanism enables a selective energy transfer to an attached spin system that acts as the quantum battery. We derive both local and global Lindblad master equations for the coupled system and identify the parameter regimes in which the engine operates efficiently. The figure of merit is the resulting steady-state population inversion of the battery, quantified in terms of ergotropy. Based on these results, we discuss the necessary conditions for practical implementations of the demon engine on realistic experimental platforms.

**Authors:** Alfonso Castillo-Gonzalez, Eric R. Bittner

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18712v2

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18712v2

**Submitted:** 2026-10-13

### Abstract

We introduce PULSUS, a direct frequency-domain framework for multidimensional spectroscopy of open quantum systems beyond the impulsive rotating-wave approximation. Coherence-frequency intervals are represented through Liouvillian resolvents, while finite Gaussian pulses are incorporated through operator-valued functions of the field-free Liouvillian. This enables direct evaluation on Cartesian grids or arbitrary spectral coordinates while retaining molecular evolution during the pulse envelopes and, when required, the complete light--matter interaction.
  The implementation is validated against analytical and independent time-domain benchmarks and supports targeted spectral sampling that reduces the number of evaluated coordinates when the response is spectrally localized. A controlled hierarchy of finite-pulse, rotating-wave, short-pulse, and impulsive models separates the errors introduced by each approximation. For a coupled electronic dimer, the total impulsive-RWA error is nonmonotonic in pulse duration: an intermediate regime provides the highest accuracy, whereas ultrashort pulses expose coupling-enabled beyond-RWA pathways and longer pulses develop substantial finite-pulse corrections. These results show that the impulsive and rotating-wave approximations cannot be assumed to improve simultaneously and should be tested when quantitatively interpreting multidimensional spectra. PULSUS provides a direct frequency-domain framework for carrying out that comparison.

### Abstract

Invertible subalgebras (ISAs) provide an alternative framework. We construct an ISA supporting chiral semions, together with its anti-semion commutant, on a square lattice with one qubit per edge. ### Abstract

Stabilizer Rényi entropy quantifies magic relative to a complete Pauli frame. In settings such as helicity states of massless particles, unsynchronized qubit registers, energy eigenbases, and systems under superselection rules, the computational basis may be fixed while the transverse Pauli axes remain unspecified. The stabilizer Rényi entropy then depends on the choice of those axes. We define a phase-averaged stabilizer Rényi entropy by averaging the Pauli moments before taking the logarithm. It is exactly invariant under independent local rephasings. For pure states, it vanishes precisely when the state is a stabilizer state in every phase convention. For a broad class of sparse supports, it reduces to a closed-form function of the basis-state probabilities alone. Within this class, the ceiling for $n$ channels is $\ln[n^{3}/(6n-5)]$ for $n\geq3$ and $\ln\frac{7}{5}$ for two channels. We apply the invariant to tree-level gluon scattering at four and five points, where the helicity-basis phases are unspecified. It saturates the two- and three-channel ceilings: $\ln\frac{7}{5}$ at four points and $\ln\frac{27}{13}$ in $3\to2$ scattering. In same-helicity $2\to3$ scattering, it lies between a floor of $\ln\frac{8}{7}$ and a ceiling of $\ln\frac{184}{125}$, reached in the resolved soft limit and particular collinear limits, respectively. In every configuration studied, the invariant is independent of the fixed external colors.

### Abstract

Quantum simulators with disordered spin Hamiltonians offer a natural route to Haar-like unitary ensembles, but face a competition. Disorder enhances randomness, yet strong disorder suppresses dynamics and localizes the system in the computational basis. This suggests an optimal intermediate disorder strength for approximating a unitary $k$-design, which has so far been identified mainly empirically. Here, we develop a perturbative theory for the moment gap $Δ^{(k)}$, the deviation of the $k$-th moment operator from its Haar value, for the full spectrum of the interacting XXZ chain in a fixed-magnetization sector. We find the non-monotonic asymptotic scalings $\ln Δ^{(k)}\sim -σ^2$ at weak disorder and $\ln Δ^{(k)}\sim -1/σ$ at strong disorder, whose crossover sets an optimal disorder strength $σ_c\sim J_{xy}$, where $J_{xy}$ is the energy scale of the non-random interaction. We further show that a multi-quench protocol further suppresses the gap exponentially in the number of quenches. Numerical simulations confirm both scalings and this exponential suppression, and we demonstrate the usefulness of the protocol for classical shadow tomography. This work suggests that strong disorder may block both thermalization and the formation of unitary designs through a common localization mechanism.

### Abstract

Under local Lindbladian evolution, the growth of conditional mutual information and its associated Markov length scale is not limited by Lieb-Robinson bounds, yet it has been unclear how quickly the Markov length can increase. ### Abstract
