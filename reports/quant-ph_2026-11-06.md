**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05865v2

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05865v2

**Submitted:** 2026-11-04

### Abstract

Quantum numbers associated with symmetries classify quantum states and identify the sectors relevant to a given simulation. Generic hardware-efficient ansatzes do not necessarily preserve the quantum numbers and may therefore explore states outside the target sector. We develop symmetry-guided algorithms based on the quantum-accessible Hilbert-Schmidt asymmetry $\mathrm{A}_\mathrm{HS}$, which quantifies the deviation of a quantum state from invariance under a specified symmetry group. This quantity guides the optimization toward a highly reduced symmetry-based target sector, without enforcing the symmetry on the preparation circuit. We demonstrate the approach as a proof of principle in variational quantum eigensolver calculations targeting singlet ground states with collective SU(2) symmetry. The applications include a five-qubit $S$-wave $p+{}^3\mathrm{He}$ two-cluster Hamiltonian, deduced from $\textit{ab initio}$ many-body calculations, and collective spin models with up to 12 particles, which provide a many-particle benchmark of asymmetry-guided optimization. Across these applications, $\mathrm{A}_\mathrm{HS}$-based guidance increases the singlet probability. Overall, the proposed algorithms improve energy accuracy during optimization and ground-state fidelity compared with direct energy minimization, with substantial fidelity improvements in the larger spin systems. These results demonstrate the utility of an asymmetry objective determined by the group action, independently of the Hamiltonian energy, for improving the reliability of variational quantum optimization.

### Abstract

We present a four-mode Fock-state photonics implementation of the variational quantum deflation (VQD) algorithm to calculate the ground-state and excited-state energies for the neutral helium atom in a CAS(2,2) Subspace. Its CAS(2,2) electronic Hilbert space is encoded into a bosonic Fock-space representation and subsequently optimized using a continuous-variable quantum circuit. The circuit architecture under study is constructed of single-mode phase shifts, beam splitters, and cross-Kerr interactions. The variational circuit is initialized in the Fock state $\ket{1100}$ and its performance is benchmarked against exact diagonalization of the active-space configuration-interaction (CASCI) Hamiltonian. We also extend the method to optimize from the first to three excited states of the helium atom with the aid of the variational quantum deflation $(VQD)$ method in a CAS(2,2). We then investigate the performance of this circuit architecture as a basis for implementing the VQD method. In the ideal noiseless simulation setting, we see that the survival probability of the ground and the excited states within the target $CAS$ subspace is unity, because the total photon number and the photon number in the two spin sectors $α$ and $β$ are preserved. We demonstrate that the proposed approach enables the determination of the ground and excited-state energies within the CAS(2,2) electronic Hilbert space using a continuous-variable quantum circuit and the variational quantum deflation ($VQD$) algorithm, while satisfying the orthogonality conditions with negligible errors.

### Abstract

Quantum algorithms for elastic media simulation have been conjectured to offer up to a quartic quantum advantage over classical methods; however, the devil is always in the details. Here, we determine key physical conditions under which this quartic separation could be achieved in the relevant parameters. For continuum elastic dynamics at fixed volume with spatial convergence order $m$, we find that the end-to-end speedup is of order $(m+4)/(m+1)$ in the simulation time, and $4/(m+1)$ in the accuracy, with exponential memory advantage. For elastic lattice systems, we define microscopically resolved bulk observables (MRBOs) as those that can be efficiently extracted from a quantum state, but cannot be resolved by a known continuum model with fewer degrees of freedom than the lattice. We then argue that the estimation of MRBOs is a candidate problem for quartic quantum advantage in problem size. We propose MRBOs for pseudorandom heterogeneous materials as an explicit end-to-end application where this occurs, while admitting efficient `quantum cheese' encodings of the material properties. We thus demonstrate how the interplay between regularity, physical scale, observable sensitivity and quantum encodability constrains the relation between classical and quantum complexity.

### Abstract

In the minimal Bell scenario, all extremal quantum correlations are known, but the Tsirelson inequalities that delimit the quantum set remain poorly understood. We study which correlations can maximize a given Tsirelson inequality, and conversely, which inequalities are maximized by a given correlation. We show that every Tsirelson inequality with quantum advantage is maximized by a single, extremal correlation, and thus self-tests the corresponding realization. **Original Title:** Nonlocal magic of fermions is bounded by entanglement fluctuations and is logarithmic for free fermions

**Authors:** Raghav G. Jha, Dengyao Luo, Alexander F. Kemper, Bojko N. Bakalov

**Link:** http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07003v1

**PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07003v1

**Submitted:** 2026-11-04


### 摘要

[Translation Failed]


### Abstract

Free-fermion states are classically simulable, yet, written as qubits, they typically carry extensive magic, the quantum resource that stabilizer circuits lack. Part of this magic is nonlocal: local unitaries acting separately on two parts of the system cannot remove it. If these local unitaries are restricted to Gaussian ones, the nonlocal magic grows linearly with the number $m$ of partially entangled fermion pairs. We show that this growth is an artifact of the Gaussian restriction: local Gaussian unitaries minimize the nonlocal stabilizer Rényi entropy for up to three pairs, but not from four pairs on. General local non-Gaussian unitaries that respect fermion parity take $m$ free-fermion pairs to fermionic Bell pairs with a fidelity that decays at most as $1/\sqrt m$ compared to exponential decay with Gaussian unitaries, so the nonlocal magic drops from linear to logarithmic in $m$. The reason is statistical --- Bell pairs have equal Schmidt weights, that is, a fully degenerate entanglement spectrum, and a state can stay far from them only if its Schmidt weights are spread over many orders of magnitude. This spread is measured by the capacity of entanglement $C_E$, the variance of the entanglement Hamiltonian, and the nonlocal magic of any fermionic state grows at most logarithmically with $C_E$. Free-fermion pairs add to $C_E$ independently, so $C_E$ grows only linearly in $m$, and this leads to the stabilizer fidelity decreasing at most as $1/\sqrt m$. Almost all the magic of free fermions, including Hartree--Fock and BCS mean-field states, is therefore local.
